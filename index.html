How does the brain's ongoing state shape its response to the world? I approach this question through intracranial stimulation, nonlinear dynamics, and brain–computer interfaces.

I am a final-year MEng student in Biomedical Engineering at Imperial College London.

My thesis asks whether pre-stimulus activity in human intracranial recordings improves the prediction of single-pulse evoked responses beyond simple signal continuation. I will also use an analog-circuit phantom with a known, built-in state effect to test whether the analysis recovers it.

Next, I plan to pursue a PhD in computational neuroscience and nonlinear dynamics at the University of Tokyo.

I am interested in three connected directions:

  • Individual brain models built with statistical physics and phase–amplitude reduction
  • Altered neural dynamics in schizophrenia
  • State-aware brain–computer interfaces
